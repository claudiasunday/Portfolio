Redesigning cancellation as a retention conversation

About the business

Dogfy Diet is fresh dog food by subscription (ES, FR, IT, DE). In a subscription business, the moment someone is about to pause or cancel is the most expensive point in the whole lifecycle. I redesigned that flow end-to-end to stop treating every cancellation the same way: the journey now detects each person's real reason and offers the concrete fix for their specific friction, while handling sensitive cases with care — pet bereavement, trial period, an upcoming charge. The result is a 5-phase flow, instrumented end-to-end, that inherits its logic from the shared backend so rules aren't duplicated between web and app.

After

The challenge

  1. It treated everyone the same. It didn't distinguish between someone leaving over price, a service failure, or because their dog had passed away. Without a reason, there's no relevant retention possible.
  2. It generated avoidable contacts to Customer Care. People were writing to support to do things the flow itself should resolve — change a date, adjust a ration.
  3. It didn't take care of sensitive scenarios. The worst of them: insisting on retaining someone whose dog had just died.

My design question: how do I reduce cancellations without manipulating anyone — offering real help and respecting the person even in their worst moment?

My role and process

I led the UX design of the flow end to end: discovery, journey mapping, defining states and edge cases, information architecture, and the binding spec for development and QA. I collaborated with Nutrition and Customer Care on contact-based retention paths, with Business on discount eligibility, and with Dev on the data contract and tracking.

Before drawing a single screen, I mapped the real flow and actively hunted for gaps: what happens with a trialer who hasn't received their box yet? What if the charge falls mid-flow? What about multiple dogs? Every gap became an explicit decision.

The turning point

The leap wasn't a prettier screen. It was reframing the problem: the reason is the product. If I don't know why the person is leaving, I can't help them — only beg them to stay.

So the flow first asks properly (macro reason → sub-reason) and branches from there. Retention stops being a generic discount and becomes the exact answer to a specific friction.

Feature ideation process

The solution: a 5-phase journey. From "are you sure you want to cancel?" to a flow that branches based on who you are and why you're leaving.

0
Validations + splash

Trialers are blocked (can't pause until 72h after receiving their WelcomeBox). Splash with a soft exit: "Stay part of Dogfy" or continue.

1
Dog selection

Only shown with more than one active dog. Out of V1.

2
Macro reason

Food and nutrition, service, price or storage, "my dog has passed away." Bereavement → jumps to the final phase.

3
Sub-reason

Narrows down the reason and decides the path: relevant retention or a direct pause.

4
Retention (happy / unhappy path)

The solution mapped to the reason. If everything is declined, the pause is confirmed. The heart of the redesign.

5
Confirmation and final states

Pause + NPS; the bereavement path with condolences; a warning about an upcoming charge with a "pause now" option.

Retention, mapped to the friction

Every reason leads to the help that actually solves that specific problem.

The ration doesn't fitRation adjustment (reuses the existing flow)
Nutritional doubtContact with Nutrition (ticket)
Service issueContact with Customer Care (ticket)
PriceDiscount, only if eligible (backend flags)
Timing / storageChange the delivery date

Retention is offered, never forced. If the person declines everything, the pause is confirmed with no added friction.
